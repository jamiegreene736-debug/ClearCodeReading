The no-pressure toolkit

Three moves for a calmer reading week.

These are observation and communication tools—not a diagnosis and not a replacement for individualized instruction.

01

Trade “try harder” for one specific observation.

Write down the kind of word, sentence, or task that caused trouble. A repeated pattern is far more useful than a label like “careless” or “behind.”

Try: “I noticed vowel teams slow things down, even in familiar words.”

02

Ask for the skill, the evidence, and the next step.

A useful progress update should connect those three things. If one is missing, ask the reading team to make the connection explicit.

Try: “Which exact skill are we building, what evidence shows progress, and what comes next?”

03

End practice while confidence is still intact.

Choose a short task connected to current instruction, stop after a small success, and note what helped. Home practice should reinforce learning—not become a second intervention session.

Try: “Let’s do five focused minutes, finish with one you know, and call that a win.”
