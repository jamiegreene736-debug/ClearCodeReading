Our Approach

The Right Starting Point for Every Student.

Your child’s learning evidence determines the program and exactly where in it they begin. Our technology helps place them in a group of their peers and keeps instruction paced to their progress.

Step One

The Assessment

Before any instruction begins, your child completes a comprehensive evaluation using assessments aligned to the intervention they’ll receive. It tells us where the breakdown is happening, and it gives you a clear picture of what comes next.

Where They Are

A precise baseline of your child’s decoding, word recognition, and spelling skills, so progress is measured against where they actually started.

What’s Getting in the Way

The specific skills that haven’t been mastered yet. Reading struggles look similar from the outside; the underlying cause often isn’t.

What It Will Take

A clear starting point in the program, the goals we’re working toward, and an honest estimate of the time and cost to get there.

Step Two

Precise Placement

Your child’s age, grade band, and assessment evidence guide which program they enter and exactly where in that program they start, so no child repeats skills they’ve already mastered or gets pushed past a gap.

Grades K–2

Orton-Gillingham

An individualized, diagnostic-prescriptive approach for students with dyslexia and related reading difficulties. Direct, multi-sensory, and cumulative, always built around the needs of the individual student. Students are placed within the scope and sequence based on assessment results.

Source: Academy of Orton-Gillingham Practitioners and Educators (ortonacademy.org)

Grades 3–8

Phonics for Reading

Developed by Dr. Anita Archer, drawing on decades of research into the Science of Reading. Explicit phonics and decoding instruction paired with fluency practice and comprehension support through decodable texts, with placement determined by assessment.

Source: Curriculum Associates (curriculumassociates.com)

The Power of a Small Group

Groups never exceed three students, matched by the skills they have in common and their age. Instruction stays targeted, and your child sits alongside peers working on exactly what they’re working on. For a kid who feels alone in their struggle, that matters.

Built-In Review

Each pathway includes sessions dedicated to review and reinforcement, so mastery is confirmed before moving forward, not assumed.

Siblings Welcome

A supervised play area means you don’t need to arrange childcare to get your reader to their session.

Step Four

How Progress Is Tracked

We capture progress data in every session and put it in front of you as it happens, so you’re never waiting on a report to know how your child is doing.

Skill Mastery

See exactly which phonics patterns and skills your child has mastered, which are in progress, and what comes next in their sequence.

Fluency Trends

Track reading rate and accuracy over time, so progress is visible as a trend line rather than a single test score.

Specialist Notes & Home Practice

Session notes from your child’s specialist, plus targeted practice activities you can use at home to reinforce what they’re working on.

When the data shows a skill isn’t sticking, instruction is adjusted. That decision is made from evidence, not guesswork.

Step Five

The Same Faces, Every Session

Many struggling readers arrive guarded, having learned to work around reading rather than through it. Our specialists build the trust that lets a child take a risk out loud, because that willingness is where real progress starts.

A Specialist Who Knows Them

The same specialist, session after session, learning which strategies work best for your child, what motivates them, and how to move them forward. They know your child can do this, and your child knows it too.

Peers in the Same Boat

Two other students working on exactly what your child is working on. For a kid who has always been the one who couldn’t, sitting with peers who get it changes how the room feels.

Belief That Shows Up in the Work

Research going back decades finds that students perform better when the adults teaching them expect they can. We hire for that conviction and give specialists the time to act on it.

Rosenthal & Jacobson, Pygmalion in the Classroom (1968)

Who Teaches Your Child

Trained Reading Specialists

Structured literacy is a specialized instructional practice. The people delivering it should be trained, supported, and here for the long haul.

Methodology-Trained

Specialists are trained in the structured literacy methodology they deliver, with ongoing supervision and professional development.

Certification Pipeline

We sponsor Orton-Gillingham coursework and supervised practicum, building certified specialists rather than competing for the few who exist.

The Same Person, Month After Month

Your child works with the same specialist over time, not whoever is available that week. Trust takes months to build and one staffing change to undo, so we invest heavily in keeping our specialists.
